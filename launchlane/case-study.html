Product case study · B2B SaaS onboarding

Make the next onboarding action clear to both sides.

LaunchLane explores a shared workspace where a SaaS implementation lead and customer administrator can see launch progress, task ownership, blockers, and the route to first value.

Evidence boundary: This is a product concept. The problem, target users, and measures below are hypotheses to validate. The prototype uses fictional accounts and has not been tested with customers; no outcome improvement is claimed.

1. Problem framing

Problem hypothesis: In B2B SaaS onboarding, implementation work can be spread across messages, spreadsheets, and separate tools. When ownership and next steps are unclear, teams may lose time coordinating and reaching the customer’s first successful workflow.

What we know: This is the design premise for the portfolio exercise, not a finding from customer interviews or production data. Discovery must confirm who experiences the problem, its frequency, and the cost of delay.

2. Users and job to be done

Primary · implementation lead

“When I am coordinating a new customer launch, help me see the shared plan, owners, progress, and blockers so I can keep both teams moving toward first value.”

Secondary · customer administrator

“When my team is setting up a new product, help me understand what we need to do next and what the implementation team is waiting on.”

JTBD use: Frame the outcome users are trying to achieve, then validate the wording and current workarounds in interviews. These are candidate jobs, not customer quotes.

3. Product objective and measures

Objective

Help eligible customer launches reach a first successful workflow with less coordination friction.

Primary outcome to validate

Median time from launch start to the customer’s first successful workflow, measured only for eligible launches with enough observation time.

Leading indicators

Milestone start-to-completion, customer invite acceptance, and blocker report-to-resolution time.

Guardrails

Reopened or incorrectly marked milestones, access-control incidents, and customer-reported confusion. Faster completion must not come at the cost of accuracy or trust.

Baseline, target, eligibility rules, and the exact “first value” event remain to be agreed with users and stakeholders. The prototype’s chart bars are illustrative, not measured results.

4. Scope and product decisions

MVP in the prototype

Shared launch overview; template-based launch creation; milestone ownership and progress; blocker reporting and resolution; customer invite preview; event and metric definitions.

Why not AI recommendations first?

The concept has no validated evidence that recommendations are the main bottleneck. Start with visibility, explicit ownership, and structured workflow; consider automation only after observing repeated decisions, reliable data, user demand, and safe human review.

Deliberately not real

No backend, authentication, enforced roles, real invitations, integrations, or production data. Role preview and invite actions are simulations.

Risks to investigate

Duplicate sources of truth; unclear customer ownership; notification overload; permission boundaries; existing onboarding systems; and whether customers will maintain a shared plan.

5. Core user flow

01 · StartCreate a launch from an agreed template.
02 · AlignIdentify implementation and customer owners.
03 · ProgressComplete setup milestones with clear next actions.
04 · UnblockRecord a blocker, owner, and resolution.
05 · Reach valueConfirm first successful workflow and review progress.

6. Example implementation-ready story

User story · proposed

As an implementation lead, I want to create a customer launch from an approved template and see the milestones and owners, so that both teams can coordinate the next steps toward first value.

Acceptance criteria: Given an authorized implementation lead, when they create a launch from an active template and provide required launch details, then a draft launch is created with the template’s milestones and assigned owners visible. Given required details are missing, the launch is not created and the missing fields are identified. Given the customer-facing invite is previewed, no email is sent until a real invite capability is deliberately implemented and approved.

This is an example story, not a claim that permissions or persistence are implemented in the static demo.

7. Validation plan before scaling

QuestionMethodSignal to look for
Where do handoffs and delays occur today?Interview implementation leads and customer admins; map a recent launch.Repeated coordination steps, unclear owners, and specific delay points.
Can users understand the shared plan?Task-based prototype usability sessions.Users can identify their next task, owner, blocker, and launch status without coaching.
Does the workflow improve onboarding?Small, permissioned pilot with a baseline and comparison plan.Directionally improved time-to-first-value with completion quality and trust guardrails intact.

Do not infer causality from a small pilot or portfolio-demo events. Define sample, time window, baseline, and operational constraints before interpreting impact.

8. Analytics and privacy

Visitors can opt in to anonymous prototype interaction events. The analytics plan excludes typed account names and blocker notes and disables click autocapture, session replay, and IP-address collection. These events describe portfolio-demo usage only; they do not represent SaaS customers, activation, or business outcomes.
