Project 03 · Product case study · Packaging scheduling

Help schedulers evaluate recovery after downtime.

A concept for comparing the current lineup with a proposed recovery, seeing affected jobs and operating trade-offs, and deciding whether to apply, adjust, or defer.

Evidence boundary: This is an independent concept using fictional orders and timings. The problem and feature gap are hypotheses, not findings about Amtech APB. No Amtech customer research, product telemetry, or business results are claimed. The mock-up is not actual APB software.
1 · Strategy + discovery

Start with the problem statement.

Public product context supports relevance; it does not establish a customer pain point or missing capability.

Problem hypothesis

After an unexpected converting-line stop, a production scheduler may need a faster way to understand schedule changes, delivery risk, setup impact, and constraints before acting. Current APB behavior and the frequency or cost of this problem must be confirmed.

Product goal

Help planners confirm a feasible recovery schedule while protecting delivery commitments and schedule stability.

Draft JTBD: “When a converting resource becomes unavailable during an active lineup, I want to understand which recovery schedule is feasible and what it changes, so I can restore production while protecting customer commitments and keeping the floor on a clear plan.”
2 · Discovery

Confirm the current workflow before proposing a feature.

Observe a recent disruption and understand what users, product configuration, and operations already support.

Research activities

  • Review current APB behavior and configuration with Product, Engineering, Support, and Implementation.
  • Interview schedulers about a specific recent event; observe planning and shift handoff where permitted.
  • Review anonymized schedule changes, event timestamps, overrides, and affected-order outcomes.

Evidence status

  • Public context: packaging scheduling and downtime response are relevant product areas.
  • Hypothesis: proposal impact may be hard to understand in the workflow.
  • Unknown: event frequency, recovery baseline, delivery impact, and data freshness.
3–4 · Prioritization + documentation

Test explanation and human review before automation.

A contained concept can learn what schedulers need to trust a recovery proposal.

Option A · visibility

Show the disruption and affected jobs

A small change, but it may not help planners compare recovery trade-offs.

Option B · concept scope

Explain and review a proposal

Compare current and proposed lineups; surface delivery, setup, constraints, and input freshness; then let an authorized planner apply, adjust, or defer.

Option C · defer

Automatically publish the new schedule

Higher operational risk before customer policy, exception handling, data quality, and safe automation boundaries are validated.

Explicit non-goals

No autonomous publishing, predictive maintenance, machine-vision integration, generic ERP redesign, or multi-option optimization without separate evidence.

Candidate MVP: current/proposed comparison; affected jobs and rationale; due-date, setup, and constraint impacts; visible input freshness; apply/adjust/defer; and safe stale-input or no-feasible-plan states.
5–6 · Prototype + breakdown

Test understanding and decision quality.

The clickable demo illustrates a workflow; it does not calculate a real schedule or change production.

Moderated tasks

  1. Identify changed jobs and the current schedule version.
  2. Explain the rationale and main trade-off.
  3. Find delivery, setup, and constraint impacts.
  4. Choose apply, adjust, or defer and explain why.
  5. Respond to stale-input and no-feasible states.

Prototype exit criteria

Schedulers can explain the comparison and current state, identify material impacts, and choose a safe next action; Engineering confirms feasibility; open data, permission, and failure questions are documented.

7 · Delivery + growth

Measure recovery without sacrificing trust.

Set targets with design partners after agreeing on baseline, definitions, data quality, and observation windows.

Primary outcome

Downtime-to-applied-feasible schedule

Elapsed time from verified disruption to successful application of a feasible schedule. A reduction target is a hypothesis until a customer baseline and threshold are agreed.

Guardrails

Feasibility, freshness, delivery, stability

No known hard-constraint violations; stale inputs are surfaced; affected-order delivery remains within agreed tolerance; schedule churn stays within agreed limits.

Candidate analytics loop: downtime verified → proposal generated → reviewed → applied / edited / deferred / failed → schedule outcome observed. Review override and defer reasons alongside speed.

Release decision

Gate rollout on evidence and safe operations.

Progress from internal validation to a design partner pilot, limited release, and wider availability only when the evidence supports it.

Readiness work

Confirm current product behavior, roles, schedule versioning, input freshness, analytics definitions, exception paths, support guidance, pilot owner, and rollback/fallback.

Expand · revise · stop

Expand only if recovery improves against baseline and delivery, feasibility, freshness, and stability guardrails hold. Revise if the problem is real but the workflow needs work. Stop or redirect if the need is uncommon, data is unreliable, or risk rises.
